# Documents

**Documents** is a sidebar page for your own files, organised in folders and shown as icons, like a file explorer. There are three document types: the **Dynamic Pad** (a free-form canvas), the **Notepad** (a page of formatted text) and the **Spreadsheet** (a grid of cells with formulas).

## Using Documents

- **New folder** creates a folder inside the folder you are viewing. Click a folder to open it; the breadcrumbs (and the up-arrow) take you back. A folder's `⋮` menu has **Rename**, **Move** (into another folder or the top level, taking everything inside it along; a folder can't go inside itself or its own sub-folders, or deeper than 8 levels) and **Delete**. Deleting a folder asks first and also deletes everything inside it (sub-folders, documents and pictures).
- **New document** opens a dropdown menu of the document types; picking one asks for a name and an optional description. The document is created in the folder you are viewing.
- Click a document icon to open its details popup: type, name, description and last-edited time, with **Edit** (name and description), **Move** (choose any folder or the top level), **Delete**, and **Open**. **Open** shows the document in **a tab of its own** beside the other workspace tabs (named after the document, with its own close button), so the **Documents** tab stays as the grid and you can keep several documents open at once and switch between them (each keeps what you have typed). Opening a document that already has a tab goes to that tab. The tab's back arrow (**Close tab**) saves any pending edits and closes it; renaming a document renames its tab, and deleting it closes its tab. The **full screen** button at the right of the editor's top bar (the corners icon) moves the same document to a full-screen view with what you have typed so far, and the same button (now **exit full screen**) returns to the tab, while Back closes it. Undo history starts afresh when the view changes. (Where the Documents page is used on its own, without tabs, a document opens inside the page and Back returns to the grid.)
- Everything **autosaves** shortly after each change (the editors show *Saved*, *Unsaved changes…*, *Saving…* or *Not saved* with a Retry button), and closing a document saves any pending edits.

### Dynamic Pad

A blank page (3000 × 2000 px to start with) holding text boxes, pictures, equations, straight lines and hand drawing. Its size, background colour and grid can be changed (see **Page setup**).

- Tools: **Select and move**, **Text box**, **Line**, **Pen**, and the **Insert** tool (a picture, a symbol or an equation, see below).
  - *Select*: click an item to select it, drag it to move it, drag its corner handle to resize (text boxes change width, pictures keep their proportions), drag a line's end handles to reposition it. Double-click a text box to edit it. Dragging the empty background scrolls the pad.
  - *Text box*: click where the text should go and type. Clicking away (or Esc) finishes; empty boxes are discarded.
  - *Line / Pen*: drag to draw. Strokes are smoothed and simplified when you let go.
  - *Pictures*: chosen with **Insert → Picture**, or **dragged from your desktop onto the pad** (browser version) to drop where you release. Pictures are shrunk to at most 1600 px and stored as PNG (up to 3 MB each, 50 per document).
- The colour swatches, **Width** and **Text** size menus apply to new items and to the selected item.
- **Undo / Redo** (also Ctrl/⌘+Z, Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z), **Delete selected** (also Delete/Backspace), and **Zoom** (25 %–200 %).
- Layers are drawn pictures first, then lines and pen strokes, then equations and text.

#### Page setup

The **page** button (the aspect-ratio icon) in the Dynamic Pad's top bar opens **Page setup**:

- **Page size**: Default (3000 × 2000 px), a paper size (A0 to A6, Letter, Legal or Tabloid), or a **Custom size**. Paper sizes can be **portrait or landscape**. The width and height are always shown and editable, in **px, in, mm or cm** (the unit menu converts what is in the boxes; pixels are 1/96 inch, as on the web, so A4 is 794 × 1123 px). A page can be 100 to 10 000 px each way. It cannot be made smaller than the items on it (the dialog says how far they reach), so nothing is cut off.
- **Background colour**: a row of ready-made colours (white, paper, cream, light blue, light green, light grey, dark grey, black) or **Other…** for any colour. Text defaults to near-black, so pick a light text colour on a dark background.
- **Grid lines**: tick **Show grid lines**, then set the **spacing** (5 to 500 px, in the chosen unit; 50 px to start with) and the **line colour**. The grid is drawn behind everything and is only a guide: items do not snap to it.
- **Reset** puts the standard page back in the dialog; nothing changes until **Apply**.

The settings are saved with the pad (a pad that was never changed stores nothing extra), shown whenever it is opened, and used by **Export**. Page changes are not part of Undo. Items cannot be moved, drawn or resized past the page's edge.

### Reloading the page

Reloading the page (Ctrl+R, F5 or the browser's button) brings back the view you had: the **workspace tabs that were open**, in the same order, with the **tab that was showing in front**, **each open document in its own tab** (reloaded from the server, so anything you had typed was already autosaved), and whether the sidebar was collapsed. A document that was deleted in the meantime closes its tab quietly. The view is remembered in the browser, per signed-in user, so another user on the same browser starts fresh. What is not remembered: where you were inside a page (the folder open in Documents, the board chosen in Boards, a project that was open), unsaved text in a dialog, scroll positions, and undo history.

### Document settings

Every open document has a **settings button** (the sliders icon, beside Export and Full screen) that opens a popup for settings that belong to that document. They are saved with the document, come back when it is opened again, and survive going full screen; **Reset** puts the standard values back in the popup, **Apply** keeps what you chose and **Cancel** changes nothing. A document with standard settings stores none.

- **Notepad**: **Pages** turns the A4 page layout on or off. On (the default) the note is an A4-wide sheet with one-inch margins and dotted page-break lines; off, the text fills the whole window with a small margin and no page lines. **Background colour** is the colour of the page (or of the whole window with pages off): nine ready-made colours or **Other…** for any. Text turns light on a dark background (and stays dark on a light one) so it remains readable; text you coloured yourself keeps its colour. Exported Word and PDF files stay white.
- **Spreadsheet**: **Gridlines** (the lines between cells), **Row and column headers** (the letters and numbers; columns are resized by their headers, so turn them back on to resize), and the **usual column width** (40–600 px) for every column you have not resized yourself. A settings change is one undo step.
- **Dynamic Pad**: the pad's page setup: size, background colour and grid (see below). It is the same popup as the page button in the pad's own toolbar.

### Notepad

A document of A4 pages with a word and character count, using the normal text-field editing of your browser. The note is a white **A4-wide page** (794 px, 8.27 in) with **one-inch margins** on a grey desk, with dark text in light and dark themes; on a narrow window the page shrinks and its side margins get smaller. The text runs on down the page, and a **dotted line labelled "Page 2", "Page 3"…** crosses it after each page's worth of text (931 px, an A4 page less its top and bottom margins) wherever more text follows, so you can see roughly where the printed pages will break. The lines are a guide that follows the text as it grows, shrinks and scrolls; they cannot be clicked, and a line of text or a picture can sit across one. The real breaks in a Word or PDF export can differ a little because fonts and pictures are measured differently there. Notes hold up to 200 000 characters. Like the Dynamic Pad, the Notepad has its **tools down the left** and the **settings of the chosen tool along the top**: **Select** (plain selecting, copying and editing; the top shows undo and redo), **Text** (the default; the top shows the text tool below) and **Insert** (a menu for a picture, symbol or equation; it works whichever tool is chosen). The save status sits at the right of the top bar, and the word and character count at the bottom.

### Spreadsheet

A grid of up to 1000 rows and 52 columns (A to AZ), starting at 50 × 12. Cells hold text, numbers or formulas; each cell can be **bold**, *italic* and aligned left, centre or right (text lines up left and numbers right unless you choose). Like the other editors it autosaves.

- **Editing**: click a cell and type to replace its contents, or press **F2** / double-click to edit what is there. **Enter** commits and moves down, **Tab** moves right (Shift reverses both), **Esc** cancels. The **formula bar** above the grid shows the active cell's contents and can be edited too. Arrow keys, Page Up/Down and Home move around; Shift extends the selection, as does dragging with the mouse. Click a column letter or row number to select the whole column or row; the corner selects everything (Ctrl+A too).
- **Formulas** start with `=`. They can use numbers, text in quotes, cell references (`B7`, `$B$7`), ranges (`A1:A10`), the operators `+ - * / ^ & %` and comparisons (`= <> < > <= >=`), brackets, and these functions: `SUM`, `AVERAGE`, `MIN`, `MAX`, `MEDIAN`, `COUNT`, `COUNTA`, `PRODUCT`, `ABS`, `INT`, `SQRT`, `POWER`, `MOD`, `ROUND`, `IF`, `AND`, `OR`, `NOT`, `LEN`, `UPPER`, `LOWER`, `TRIM` and `CONCAT`. Errors show as `#DIV/0!`, `#VALUE!`, `#NAME?`, `#REF!` (a deleted cell), `#CIRC!` (a formula that depends on itself) or `#ERROR!` (a formula that cannot be read). Whole-column ranges such as `A:A` are not supported.
- **Rows and columns**: the toolbar inserts rows above or below and columns left or right of the selection, and deletes the selected rows or columns. Formulas follow their cells, and a reference to a deleted cell becomes `#REF!`. Drag the edge of a column letter to change the column's width.
- **Copy, cut and paste**: Ctrl+C / X / V work on a block of cells. Inside Keening formulas and looks are kept (formulas are copied as written, not adjusted). Other programs get the shown values as tab-separated text, and tab-separated text from elsewhere (such as Excel) pastes into cells. One copied value fills a larger selection. Pasting past the edge adds rows and columns, up to the limits.
- **Undo and redo**: Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y (or Ctrl+Shift+Z) cover edits, formatting, rows and columns, column widths and imports. Delete clears the selected cells.
- A sheet can hold up to 20 000 filled cells and 1000 characters per cell; pictures cannot be added.

### Importing and exporting

- **Import**: the **Import** button (upload arrow) sits beside **Export** at the top right of an open Spreadsheet or Notepad. It replaces what is in the document with the file you choose, after asking if the document is not empty; the replacement is one undo step (Ctrl+Z).
  - **Spreadsheet: CSV files**. Commas, semicolons or tabs are recognised as the separator, quoted values may contain separators and line breaks, UTF-8 files (with or without a byte order mark) and older Windows (Latin-1) files are read, and files up to 5 MB are accepted. Values that read as numbers become numbers; text that starts with `=` becomes a formula. Anything beyond 1000 rows or 52 columns is left out, and Keening says so. (To start a spreadsheet from a CSV, create an empty Spreadsheet and import into it.)
  - **Notepad: Word (`.docx`), PDF and plain text (`.txt`) files**, up to 25 MB; the kind is worked out from what is in the file. A **Word** file keeps its text, headings, bold, italic, underline, colour, highlight, subscript and superscript, relative text sizes (a heading's size compared with the body text), bullet and numbered lists (numbers become typed numbers, nesting becomes indents), indents, tabs, tables (a row becomes a line with tabs between the cells) and **pictures** (PNG, JPEG, GIF, BMP and WebP; they are shrunk and stored with the note like any inserted picture, up to the note's limit of pictures). Left out: headers and footers, footnotes, text boxes, equations written in Word's own format, pictures in other formats, and layout such as columns and page breaks. A **PDF** gives its text only: lines and paragraph gaps are rebuilt from where the text sits on the page, so a table comes out as lines of text and columns are read in the order they were drawn; pictures and layout are not imported, and PDFs that are scans (pictures of text) or password-protected are refused with an explanation. A **text** file may be UTF-8 (with or without a byte order mark), UTF-16 or Latin-1. Older `.doc` files are refused (save them as `.docx` first). Text beyond 200 000 characters is cut at a line end and Keening says so, as it does for any picture that could not be added.
- **Export**: every document opens with an **Export** button (download arrow, top right) beside the document's name:

  | Document type | Export formats |
  | --- | --- |
  | Spreadsheet | CSV (comma separated, UTF-8 with a byte order mark so Excel reads accents and symbols; values, with formulas worked out, up to the last used row and column) |
  | Notepad | Word (`.docx`), PDF and plain text (`.txt`) |
  | Dynamic Pad | PDF, either **the whole page** (the pad page's size, such as A4, with its background colour and grid) or **cropped to the items** (one page just big enough for them, with a margin; the background and grid are kept) |

  In the export dialog choose the **file type**, the **file name** (illegal characters are replaced) and the **folder**: **Choose folder…** opens the system's folder chooser and the file is written straight into that folder; the folder is remembered for the rest of the session and an existing file is never replaced (a number is added to the name). Only Chromium browsers (Chrome, Edge, Opera, Brave…) can ask for a folder, and they refuse a few folders such as Downloads, Documents and Desktop themselves (choose a sub-folder of them); in Firefox and Safari the browser downloads the file into its normal downloads folder instead, and the dialog says so.
- **What a Notepad export keeps**: Word and PDF keep text size, colour, highlight, bold, italic, underline, subscript and superscript, indents, bullets and tabs, and pictures; equations are drawn as pictures (a Word reader sees the LaTeX as the picture's alt text). Plain text keeps the words, indents and bullets, with `[picture]` for a picture and an equation as `$LaTeX$`. A PDF or Word file of a Notepad is A4 with 1-inch margins and runs over as many pages as it needs, as the note's page breaks show. A Dynamic Pad PDF draws text, lines and hand drawing as vectors and pictures and equations as images, in the same layers as on the pad (pictures, then lines and drawing, then equations and text). PDFs use the Roboto font, which covers Latin, Greek and Cyrillic letters and many symbols; a character it does not have (an emoji, for instance) prints as an empty box.

### The insert tool

One insert tool (the **+** box button, in the tool column down the left of both a Dynamic Pad and a Notepad) is shared by both document types. It never takes keyboard focus and keeps the caret and selection where they were. Its menu has:

- **Picture**: choose an image file (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP or BMP). It is shrunk to at most 1600 px, stored as PNG and uploaded once. In a Dynamic Pad it becomes a movable, resizable picture; in a Notepad it appears in the text at the caret (shown at most 360 × 300 px).
- **Symbol**: a picker with Common, Math, Greek, Arrows, Shapes, Currency and Super/subscript groups, and a **Recent** group. In a Notepad (or a Dynamic Pad text box being edited) the symbol goes in at the caret, replacing any selection, as its own undo step; in a Dynamic Pad with no text box being edited it makes a new text box holding the symbol.
- **Equation**: an editor for equations written in LaTeX, with building-block buttons in groups (**Tables**: ready-made column and row vectors, 2×2 and 3×3 matrices, determinants, piecewise "cases", aligned equations, grid tables and binomials, plus a **Build a table or matrix…** button that opens a builder where you choose the number of rows and columns, the edges (brackets, parentheses, braces, bars, double bars, none or grid lines) and type each cell; tick "Cells are words" to show them upright, as in a vector of named quantities such as age and mileage, or untick it to write each cell as maths; **Common**: fractions, powers, roots, sums, integrals, limits, brackets, matrices; **Greek** (with ready-made hats such as ϕ̂ and β̂); **Accents**: hat, wide hat, tilde, bar, vector arrow, dots, check, breve, acute, grave, underline and braces above or below (each puts the caret inside its braces, so the next symbol you choose goes under the accent: **Accents → x̂**, then **Greek → ϕ** gives `hat{phi}`); **Functions**: argmin, argmax, min, max, sup and inf (each leaves a gap underneath for what they range over: **Functions → argmin**, then **Greek → ϕ** gives `\underset{\phi}{\operatorname{argmin}}`), plus sin, cos, tan, log, ln, exp, det, Pr, expected value, variance and an **op** button for your own named operator; **Operators**; **Sets**: intersection, union, element of / not an element of, subset, subset or equal, proper subset, not a subset, superset, empty set, set difference, symmetric difference, complement, Cartesian product, power set, cardinality, indexed unions and intersections, set-builder braces, ℕ ℤ ℚ ℝ ℂ and ℵ; **Logic**: for all, there exists, not, and, or, implies, if and only if, proves, models, therefore and more) and a live preview. An equation wider than the preview box scrolls sideways inside it: a scrollbar appears along the bottom of the preview, and you can drag the equation (with the mouse too), drag the scrollbar, or use a sideways wheel or trackpad swipe. The table builder's cells and preview scroll the same way. Hover a button for its name; the caret lands where you still need to type. **Curly braces**: in LaTeX a plain { } only groups things and is not drawn, so to show braces write { and } (the **{ }** button in Common does, and **{ } ↕** stretches to fit what is inside); the editor reminds you under the text box. Common also has angle brackets, floor, ceiling and norm. Invalid equations cannot be inserted (equations can be up to 2000 characters). In a Dynamic Pad the equation becomes an item you can move, resize (proportions are kept), recolour with the colour swatches, **double-click to edit** and delete like any other. In a Notepad it is drawn in the text; click it to edit.

In a Notepad a picture is laid out with its top edge level with the top of its line, so the text caret sits at the picture's top-left corner (just before it) or top-right corner (just after it); click the left or right half of a picture to put the caret on that side. The line holding a picture is as tall as the picture, so the text on the lines below starts underneath it, never behind it. **Double-click a picture to crop it**, in a Notepad or on a Dynamic Pad. The crop menu shows the whole picture with a box you drag by its corners and edges (or move by dragging inside it) to choose what to keep; the rest is dimmed. Shape buttons lock the box to Original, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2 or 16:9 (Free lets you choose any shape), the size in pixels is shown below, **Reset** puts the whole picture back, and **Crop** keeps the chosen part. The cropped copy is saved as a new picture and takes the old one's place (on a pad at the same scale, so it covers just the kept part at the same size as before); the original stays until the document no longer uses it, so Ctrl+Z brings it back. Clicking a picture once still just selects it or places the caret. **Drag a picture (or an equation) to move it** to another place in the text: the caret follows the pointer to show where it will land, and dropping outside the page leaves it where it was. The move is one undo step. In a Notepad a picture or equation takes one placeholder character of the note's text, so it moves, copies and deletes with the text around it, is undone with Ctrl+Z, and is not counted as a word. Up to 100 pictures and equations per note.

### Copy, cut and paste

- **Notepad**: Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V (and the right-click menu) keep **formatting, equations and pictures**. Other apps receive plain text (an equation as its LaTeX, a picture left out). Pasting into another note adds the pictures to that note. Pasting text that came from somewhere else is plain text, and stray placeholder characters in typed or pasted text are dropped. A paste is one undo step.
- **Dynamic Pad**: select an item (text box, picture, equation, line or pen stroke) and use Ctrl+C / Ctrl+X / Ctrl+V, or the **copy** and **paste** buttons on the top bar. **Ctrl+D** duplicates the selected item. Each paste lands 24 px further down and right so copies do not hide each other, and pictures pasted into another pad are added to that pad. Other apps receive a text box's text or an equation's LaTeX; text copied elsewhere pastes as a new text box.

- **Pictures from the clipboard**: a screenshot or any picture you have copied (from a web page, an image editor, Snipping Tool and so on) pastes with Ctrl+V or the paste button: into a Notepad at the caret, or onto a Dynamic Pad as a new picture. It is shrunk to 1600 px and stored like any other picture. If the clipboard also has text, the text is pasted instead of the picture. A picture copied inside Keening is also put on the system clipboard as a picture, so it can be pasted into other programs, and pasting it back into the same document reuses the stored picture instead of uploading it again. The browser may ask permission to see the clipboard the first time; if it will not allow it, Keening says so.
- **Between the two**: an equation (or picture, or text) copied in a Dynamic Pad pastes into a Notepad as an equation (picture, text), and a lone equation or picture copied in a Notepad pastes into a pad as an equation or picture; other text pastes as a text box. If both have a copy that matches the clipboard, the newer one is used.
- **Feedback and the browser**: copying and cutting show "Copied" / "Cut". Reading the clipboard needs the browser's permission (Chrome asks the first time). If the browser refuses, Keening pastes its own last copy instead; if there is none it tells you to allow clipboard access for the site (the icon next to the address bar). If the browser refuses to *write* the clipboard, the message says the copy stays inside Keening.

### The text tool

One text tool is shared by Notepads and Dynamic Pad text boxes. From left to right: **undo** and **redo**, **bold**, *italic* and underline, a text **size** (the − and + buttons step through 10–96), a **text colour** and a **highlight colour** (each opens the colour picker, with a stripe under the icon showing the colour in use), and **subscript** and **superscript** (smaller text lowered or raised, **Ctrl+-** and **Ctrl++** (Ctrl+= works too, and Ctrl+, / Ctrl+. as in Google Docs), which replace the browser's zoom shortcuts while the text has focus (the page can still be zoomed with the browser's menu, or by clicking outside the text); they are a property of the characters, separate from inserted equations, and turn each other off), and **decrease / increase indent**. It never takes keyboard focus, so the caret and selection stay where they are.

- **Keyboard shortcuts**: Ctrl/Cmd+**B** (bold), **I** (italic) and **U** (underline) toggle the style of the selected text, or of what you type next at the caret, in a Notepad and in a text box being edited. With a text box selected (not being edited) they style the whole box. Pressing one again turns it off.
- **Selected text** (in a Notepad, or while editing a text box) takes the change; the bar shows what the selection looks like (a mix shows only what every selected character shares). With **nothing selected**, the choice applies to what you type next at the caret.
- **Colour picker**: drag the square (saturation across, brightness down) and the hue slider, or type a hex colour, then **Apply**. **Default colour** (text) or **No highlight** clears the colour. The star saves the current colour as a **favourite** (up to 24); **Recently used** shows the last 12 colours applied. Tapping a favourite or a recent colour applies it straight away. Both lists are shared by every colour picker and remembered in this browser.
- **Indent** with the caret after some text on a line adds a tab at the caret, so only what follows it moves and the text before it stays put; the tab is a single character (one Backspace removes it) drawn out to the next tab stop, and tab stops are the same distance from the left edge on every line, so indents after text line up from line to line (a caret at the start of a line, in its indent, or just after a bullet moves the whole line, as does a selection). Otherwise it moves every line touched by the selection (or the caret's line) by four spaces; **decrease** removes up to four spaces (or a tab). The spaces are ordinary text and take the line's text size. **Tab** indents and **Shift+Tab** outdents the same way as the buttons (in a Notepad and in a Dynamic Pad text box being edited), instead of moving to the next control. An indent acts as one piece: **Backspace** and **Delete** remove the whole indent (not one space), and the caret and selections skip over it instead of stopping inside it. Fewer than four spaces at the start of a line, or spaces elsewhere in a line, are ordinary spaces. In a Dynamic Pad, with a text box selected (not being edited) it indents the whole box. Indenting stops rather than pass a document's character limit. **A long indented line or bullet wraps with a hanging indent**: the lines it runs onto start under the first character of the text (after the bullet, for a bullet), not back at the left edge. This is done by a modified copy of Flutter's text field in `lib/forked` (see its README).
- **Bullet points**: the bullet button (or Ctrl+Shift+8 / Ctrl+Shift+L) turns the selected lines, or the line the caret is on, into bullet points ("• " in front of the text, after any indent); pressing it again, or when every selected line is already a bullet, turns them back into plain lines. The button lights up when the lines are bullets. **Enter** at the end of a bullet starts the next bullet at the same indent (in the middle of one it splits the line), and **Enter on an empty bullet ends the list**. A bullet acts as one piece like an indent: Backspace or Delete next to it removes the whole bullet, and the caret and selections never stop between the dot and the space. **Tab** and **Shift+Tab** nest and un-nest bullets. It works in a Notepad and in a Dynamic Pad text box (with a box selected rather than edited, it changes every line).
- **Undo / redo** step back through typing (a burst of typing is one step), formatting and indenting. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y in the text use the same history. In a Dynamic Pad, while a text box is being edited they act on that text; with a box selected they step the pad's own history, and in text mode with nothing selected there is nothing to undo.
- Choices made at the caret are remembered across documents: the next empty Notepad, or the next new Dynamic Pad text box, starts with the last format picked.
- In a **Dynamic Pad** the text tool floats over the top-left of the pad whenever the **Text box** tool is active, a text box is selected, or one is being edited. In text mode with nothing selected it sets how new text boxes start; with a text box selected (not editing) the change applies to the whole box. The drawing colour and width controls are shown for the other tools.
- New text takes the format of the text before it (or of the first character when typed at the start); replacing a selection keeps the replaced text's format.

## Adding another document type

Types are listed in `documentTypes` in `lib/pages/documents_page.dart` (name, icon and a one-line description shown in the chooser). The server's allowed types are `KINDS` in `cloud-run/src/pads.js`, and `pads.kind` is checked by migrations `16-document-folders.sql` and `18-sheets.sql` (the latter adds `sheet`; run `scripts/apply-schema.ps1` against the database before deploying the API that uses it). A new type also needs its own editor screen and save validation.

## Storage and limits

Documents are stored in the `pads` table (kept under its original name so existing pads carry over as Dynamic Pads), with a `kind` (`pad`, `notepad` or `sheet`), a `folder_id`, a description (migration `15-pad-descriptions.sql`) and a small JSON `doc`: a Dynamic Pad's layout, a Notepad's `{text, runs}`, or a Spreadsheet's `{rows, cols, cells, widths}`. Formatting is stored as **runs**: sorted, non-overlapping ranges of the text (`start`, `end`, and any of `size` 8–200, `color`, `bg` highlight, `bold`, `italic`, `underline`). Dynamic Pad text boxes carry the same `runs` on top of their own size and colour. The server rebuilds runs from those fields only and rejects ranges outside the text (up to 5000 runs per note, 500 per text box). Folders are in `pad_folders` (nested, up to 8 levels); deleting a folder cascades to its sub-folders and documents. Pictures (in pads and in notes) are stored once in `pad_images`, so autosaves send only the layout or text. A Notepad's runs can also carry an `embed` (`{type: 'image', imageId}` or `{type: 'equation', latex}`) on a placeholder character (U+FFFC); the server checks that the run covers only placeholder characters, that a picture exists in that note, and that equations are 1–2000 characters. Dynamic Pad equations are items `{type: 'equation', x, y, w, h, latex, color}`. A pad's optional `page` is `{width, height, background, grid: {show, size, color}}` (pixels, 100–10 000 for the size and 5–500 for the grid spacing, `#RRGGBB` colours); the server validates it and a pad without one has the standard page. A spreadsheet is stored sparsely (with an optional `settings` of `{gridlines, headers, colWidth}`, and a Notepad's `doc` an optional `settings` of `{pages, background}`; the server validates both and untouched documents store none): `cells` maps names such as `B7` to `{v, b, i, a}` (what was typed, a formula when it starts with `=`; bold; italic; alignment `l`, `c` or `r`) and `widths` maps column letters to widths of 40–600 px. The server rebuilds it from those fields only, checks every cell is inside `rows` × `cols`, and rejects over-long cells or too many cells.

Authenticated `POST /pads` accepts `listPads`, `createPad` (with `kind` and `folderId`), `renamePad`, `updatePad`, `movePad`, `deletePad`, `getPad`, `savePad`, `uploadImage`, `getImage`, `listFolders`, `createFolder`, `renameFolder`, `moveFolder` and `deleteFolder`. Every action checks ownership. Dynamic Pads and Notepads accept pictures; spreadsheets do not. The route allows bodies up to 6 MB for picture uploads; all other routes keep their small limit.

Limits: 500 documents and 200 folders per user; Dynamic Pads hold up to 3000 items and 150 000 pen points (layouts up to 1.5 MB, text boxes up to 10 000 characters); Notepads up to 200 000 characters; spreadsheets up to 1000 rows, 52 columns, 20 000 filled cells and 1000 characters per cell. The server rebuilds every saved document from whitelisted fields and rejects anything out of range. Pictures no longer used by their document are removed an hour after the next save, so undo still works for a while.

## Search

The global search (the search button on the dashboard) finds folders and documents by name, description and folder, and also looks **inside Notepads and spreadsheets** (their text and cell values); such a result shows the start of the text. Picking a folder opens it on the Documents page; picking a document opens its folder and shows the document's details popup. Search keeps working if the document tables are missing (before migrations 14–16 are applied), it just returns no documents.
